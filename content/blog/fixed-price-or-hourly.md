Fixed price or hourly? When you hire a developer, it is one of the first things
to settle. My rule fits in one sentence, and it is the same one on my
[How I work](/how-i-work#faq-pricing) page:

> I work fixed price when the scope is fixed, and hourly for staff augmentation,
> code reviews, or when the work is open-ended.

Below I explain what that means for you: how to tell which side your project is
on, what happens when the scope changes, and what stays the same either way. The
prices themselves live on [Services](/catalog), so I don't repeat them here.

## When does a fixed price fit?

A fixed price needs a scope you can write down: what gets built, and what does
not. Then you get one number before any work starts, and you can plan your
budget around it.

That is how the [SaaS MVP build](/catalog/zero-to-production-saas-mvp) works.
Its price and its timeline say "from", because both depend on the scope, and you
get a quote for your scope before any work starts. The build opens with a short
discovery sprint: a system architecture diagram, a tech stack recommendation
with the reasons, an infrastructure cost estimate, the main risks, and a phased
build roadmap with timeline estimates. The service page also says what the price
leaves out. Integrations beyond Stripe, and features added after launch, are
quoted once we scope them.

The [code and security audit](/catalog/codebase-health-audit) is fixed price
too: three days of review, a written report with a prioritized fix roadmap, and
a call to walk you through it. Fixing what it finds is a separate job, quoted as
a build or as ongoing work.

So a fixed price suits you when:

- you know what the first version must do,
- the work has an end, such as a launch or a report,
- you want the cost settled before you commit.

## When does hourly fit?

Hourly fits work without a fixed edge. My rule names three kinds: staff
augmentation, where I join your team; code reviews; and work that is open-ended.
A fixed quote for that would be a guess, and one of us would pay for the guess.

That is the [Ongoing](/catalog/cto-advisory-retainer) service: architecture
decisions, code review, technical direction for your developers, and looking
after what is already in production. It has two ways to pay. Pay by the hour
when the need comes and goes, or take a monthly retainer when it is steady. The
retainer works out cheaper from about 20 hours a month.

### Hourly, when AI agents do the typing

I work with AI coding agents, so a fair question is whether you pay while they
run. You don't. I bill only the hours I actually work on your project: setting
the agents' tasks, checking their progress and reports, judging the quality,
writing new rules, and calls. Once I hand the agents a batch of work, the timer
stops. It starts again only when they have finished and I am back on your
project. The full answer is on
[Why not just AI](/why-not-just-ai#faq-hourly-while-ai-works).

## What happens when the scope changes?

The rule is short: if the scope changes once we have started, you get a quote
for the change before I start on it. No surprise costs.

On a build you see working software every week, with a short written update. So
you always know where the project stands, instead of finding out at the end what
the fixed price bought you.

## Why does every engagement start small?

A build and ongoing work both start with a small first milestone: one or two
weeks of work. If either of us wants to stop at the end of it, we stop, and you
keep everything built so far. On a build, the discovery sprint is part of that
milestone, so you keep the diagram, the estimates and the roadmap too.

You don't have to commit to months of work before we know the collaboration is
right. That holds whether the rest is a fixed price or hourly.

## What stays the same either way?

The way you pay changes the invoice, not what you can hold me to:

1. **A five-day refund.** If in the first five days you feel it is not working,
   tell me and I refund what you paid. This one covers the strategy session and
   the audit too.
2. **A small first milestone**, on a build and on ongoing work, as above.
3. **Full ownership from day one.** The code, the accounts, the servers and the
   keys are in your name, on a build and on ongoing work.
4. **Free bug fixes for 30 days** on a build: bugs in what I delivered are fixed
   on my time.

All five promises, in the order a project meets them, are on
[How I work](/how-i-work).

## Not sure which side your project is on?

That is normal when the idea is still forming. You can
[send a written brief](/book#brief): a paragraph about your idea or problem, and
I write back with 3 concrete architectural improvements. No cost, no pitch. If
you want a full hour on it, the paid [strategy session](/catalog/strategy-call)
gives you honest technical feedback and a plan you can act on, with or without
me.

Or [book a free 30-minute call](/book), tell me what you are building, and we
work out which one fits.
